01 / A LITTLE ABOUT ME

More than just the code.

I have always been interested in the bigger picture of building software — not only whether something works, but whether it makes sense, feels intuitive and solves the right problem.

My interests sit across product design, user experience, frontend development and backend implementation. I enjoy understanding how the different pieces fit together rather than looking at development as a series of disconnected tasks.

ActiveEdge gave me an opportunity to put that approach into practice. I contributed to the design and development process, from wireframes and product design through to frontend work and Supabase database configuration.

This portfolio is a record of that involvement. It brings together the evidence behind the work, the concepts I engaged with and what I am taking forward into future projects.

MY REFLECTION PERSONAL LEARNING

One thing this part of the project made me think about is how important it is to establish expectations early. Because I joined after the original team charter had been created, I wasn't involved in putting that initial agreement together. That distinction is important, but it also gave me a reason to consider the value of having a shared understanding before development gets too far underway.

A team charter is useful because it gives people a common reference point. It sets out how a team intends to work together, rather than leaving every expectation open to interpretation. In future projects, I would make a point of familiarising myself with those expectations as early as possible and contributing to them where appropriate.

MY REFLECTION PERSONAL LEARNING

User stories are a useful reminder that software is ultimately being built for someone. It is easy to get caught up in the technical side of a project and focus on what needs to be coded, but the user's perspective helps explain why that functionality matters in the first place.

I see the value in breaking requirements down into smaller, understandable pieces of work. It makes the development process easier to reason about and gives the team a clearer idea of what needs to be achieved. Going forward, I want to be more deliberate about connecting interface and development decisions back to the needs behind each requirement.

MY REFLECTION PERSONAL LEARNING

A backlog is only useful if it helps the team make sense of the work ahead. Having tasks and requirements organised in one place creates visibility and makes it easier to understand what still needs attention.

I also think prioritisation is one of the more important parts of this process. Not every task has the same urgency or impact, and being able to distinguish between them helps prevent a team from spending time in the wrong places. It is something I want to carry into my own development projects, especially when balancing design decisions, technical requirements and deadlines.

MY REFLECTION PERSONAL LEARNING

Sprint planning highlights the difference between having a list of things that need to be done and having a realistic plan for actually doing them. A sprint needs direction. The work selected should contribute towards a goal, and the tasks should be clear enough for the team to understand what progress looks like.

What I take from this is the importance of being realistic about the amount of work involved. Development can be unpredictable, particularly when design and technical implementation overlap. In future, I would pay close attention to breaking work into manageable tasks and making sure that progress can be understood by the rest of the team.

MY REFLECTION PERSONAL LEARNING

The logbook is important because it gives the project a record beyond the final product. It is easy to look back at completed work and forget the planning, decisions and smaller steps that helped get there. Documentation makes that process more visible.

It also reinforces the idea that development is not just about the end result. Being able to look back at what was recorded helps create a better understanding of how work progressed. I want to keep improving the way I document my own work, because clear records are useful not only for assessment, but also for communication, accountability and future maintenance.

07 / PERSONAL DEVELOPMENT

What I take forward.

ActiveEdge gave me the opportunity to contribute across different parts of a software development project. My involvement included wireframing, product design, frontend refinement and Supabase database configuration.

One of the things I value about working across these areas is seeing how closely they are connected. Design decisions influence implementation. The structure of a database affects how information can be used. Frontend decisions influence how someone experiences the product.

It has reinforced the way I like to approach development: understanding the product as a whole, rather than focusing on one isolated part of it.

I also recognise that there is always room to improve. Better planning, clearer communication and consistent documentation all contribute to a stronger development process. These are things I want to continue developing alongside my technical skills.

MY BIGGEST TAKEAWAY
✳

Good development is about seeing the whole picture.

The strongest products come from bringing together thoughtful design, clear requirements, sound technical decisions and an understanding of the people using them.
